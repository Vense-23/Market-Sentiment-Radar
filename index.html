1. 宏观与市场情绪

People are confusing an oil-driven macro shock with an AI bubble. The war drives oil higher, which pushes inflation up, increases the risk of Fed rate hikes, drives borrowing costs higher, and reduces consumer spending. Higher yields also mean future cash flows get discounted more aggressively, crushing growth stock valuations. The word recession starts getting throw around, Michael Burry starts to look like he's onto something, and price drives narratives so we start getting the most absurd narratives on this being an AI bubble, and AI financing, and it has no use, no ROI. Meanwhile, the actual AI buildout is stronger than ever.
翻译：人们正在将石油驱动的宏观冲击与人工智能泡沫混淆。战争推高油价，从而推高通胀，增加了美联储加息的风险，推高了借贷成本，并减少了消费者支出。更高的收益率也意味着未来现金流会以更激进的方式折现，从而压垮成长股的估值。衰退这个词开始被提及，迈克尔·伯里看起来像是有所发现，价格驱动叙事，因此我们开始听到关于这是一场人工智能泡沫、人工智能融资、以及它没有用处、没有投资回报率等等最荒谬的叙事。与此同时，实际的人工智能建设比以往任何时候都更加强大。

  • NWG (NatWest Group plc)
    Anyone been taking a look at the Royal Bank of Scotland these past couple of years? They were the largest global bank to almost end up bust in 2008 but were saved at the taxpayer's expense in the UK. It was a long road to recovery but the UK government has now sold all of it's shares in the bank since 2025. They then rebranded to "natwest group" but in reality it's literally still the same scottish entity, just with a fresh paint. Their stock is only at a market cap of 45bn as of right now. They have had a huge sell off since their earnings report even though the report was mainly a hit. They now trade at ridiculous lows of 7.8x p/e whilst being one of the most efficient banks there is. What makes this even crazier is that this is literally one of the safest plays you can possibly make.
